# 02 — Nutrition: The 80%

Nutrition is the lever that decides whether you lose fat. Training decides whether the weight you lose is fat or muscle. Get both right.

---

## Step 1 — Estimate maintenance calories (TDEE)

We use the **Mifflin-St Jeor** equation (the most accurate of the common formulas), then apply an activity multiplier.

### BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate)

**Men:** `BMR = (10 × weight kg) + (6.25 × height cm) − (5 × age) + 5`
**Women:** `BMR = (10 × weight kg) + (6.25 × height cm) − (5 × age) − 161`

> Weight in kg = lbs ÷ 2.205. Height in cm = inches × 2.54.

### TDEE = BMR × activity multiplier

| Activity level | Multiplier |
|----------------|-----------|
| Sedentary (desk job, little movement) | 1.2 |
| Lightly active (training 5–6×/wk counts here at minimum) | 1.375 |
| Moderately active (training + on your feet a lot) | 1.55 |
| Very active (physical job + training) | 1.725 |

Since you train **5–6 days/week**, you're at **1.375–1.55**. Start at **1.4** and let real-world data correct it (Step 4).

---

## Worked example

A 30-year-old man, 90 kg (198 lb), 180 cm (5'11"):

```
BMR  = (10 × 90) + (6.25 × 180) − (5 × 30) + 5
     = 900 + 1125 − 150 + 5
     = 1880 kcal

TDEE = 1880 × 1.4 = ~2632 kcal/day  ← maintenance
```

**Plug in your own numbers above.** This is your starting estimate, not gospel — Step 4 refines it.

---

## Step 2 — Set your deficit

For an advanced lifter cutting, a **moderate deficit of ~20% below maintenance** is the sweet spot: fast enough to see progress, slow enough to hold muscle.

```
Cutting calories = TDEE × 0.80
```

Using the example: `2632 × 0.80 ≈ 2100 kcal/day`.

**Rules:**
- Aim to lose **0.5–1.0% of bodyweight per week**. For a 90 kg person that's ~0.45–0.9 kg/week.
- **Never** crash below ~12 kcal/lb of bodyweight for extended periods — that's where muscle loss and hormonal problems start.
- If you're already lean (<12% BF), use a **15% deficit** instead of 20%.

---

## Step 3 — Set your macros

Macros are set in this priority: **protein → fat → carbs fill the rest.**

### Protein — the muscle-protector (set first)
- **1.0 g per lb of bodyweight** (≈ 2.2 g/kg). Slightly higher in a deficit is protective.
- Example: 198 lb → **~200 g protein/day**.
- Spread across **4–5 meals**, each with ~40–50 g, to keep muscle-protein synthesis elevated.

### Fat — for hormones (set second)
- **0.3–0.4 g per lb of bodyweight**, never below ~0.25 g/lb (testosterone needs dietary fat).
- Example: 198 lb → **~70 g fat/day**.

### Carbs — for training performance (fill the rest)
Carbs get whatever calories remain. They fuel your heavy sessions, so don't fear them.

```
Calories from protein = 200 g × 4 = 800 kcal
Calories from fat      = 70 g × 9  = 630 kcal
Remaining for carbs    = 2100 − 800 − 630 = 670 kcal
Carbs                  = 670 ÷ 4 = ~167 g/day
```

### Your daily targets (example)
| Macro | Grams | Calories |
|-------|-------|----------|
| Protein | 200 g | 800 |
| Fat | 70 g | 630 |
| Carbs | 167 g | 670 |
| **Total** | | **~2100 kcal** |

**Fiber:** aim for **30–40 g/day** — keeps you full and digestion regular on a cut.
**Water:** **3–4 L/day**. Hunger is often thirst, and hydration drives performance.

---

## Step 4 — Auto-correct with real data (this is what coaches actually do)

The formula is a **starting estimate**. Your body gives you the real answer:

1. Eat your target calories consistently for **10–14 days**.
2. Track **morning bodyweight daily**, take the **weekly average** (daily weight is noisy — water, sodium, glycogen).
3. Compare week-over-week average:

| Weekly average change | Action |
|-----------------------|--------|
| Lost 0.5–1.0% bodyweight | Perfect — hold calories. |
| Lost nothing / gained | Drop calories by ~150–200 (cut from carbs/fat, never protein). |
| Lost >1.5% | Too fast — add ~150 calories back to protect muscle. |

This feedback loop matters more than any formula. **Adjust every 2 weeks based on the trend, not a single day.**

## Meal structure (example day, ~2100 kcal)

This is a **template** — swap foods you like, keep the macros similar.

| Meal | Example | ~Protein |
|------|---------|----------|
| Breakfast | 4 eggs + 50 g oats + berries | 35 g |
| Lunch | 200 g chicken breast + 150 g rice + veg | 50 g |
| Pre/post workout | Whey shake + banana | 30 g |
| Dinner | 200 g lean beef or salmon + potatoes + salad | 50 g |
| Evening | 200 g Greek yogurt / casein + nuts | 30 g |

### Best food choices on a cut
- **Protein:** chicken/turkey breast, lean beef, white fish, salmon, eggs/egg whites, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, whey/casein.
- **Carbs:** rice, potatoes, oats, fruit, whole grains, legumes. High-volume veg (broccoli, greens, peppers) for fullness.
- **Fats:** olive oil, nuts, avocado, fatty fish, whole eggs. Use sparingly — fat is calorie-dense (9 kcal/g).

---

## Refeeds & diet breaks (the sustainability tools)

Cutting for many weeks lowers energy, performance, and hunger hormones (leptin). We manage this:

- **Refeed days:** 1–2 days/week at maintenance, with the extra calories coming from **carbs**. Restores glycogen and training performance, gives a psychological break.
- **Diet breaks:** every **6–8 weeks of cutting**, take **1 week at maintenance calories**. This isn't "cheating" — it restores hormones, reduces fatigue, and improves *long-term* fat loss. Then resume the deficit.

A planned, structured break beats an unplanned binge every time.
