# 05 — Cardio & Recovery

## Cardio: a tool, not the main event

Fat loss is driven by your **calorie deficit**, which comes mostly from **diet**. Cardio is a way to *widen* that deficit and improve heart health — it is **not** a way to out-train a bad diet. We use it strategically so it burns fat without eating into your recovery or muscle.

### The hierarchy (use in this order)

1. **Daily steps (NEAT)** — the most underrated fat-loss tool. Set a target of **8,000–12,000 steps/day**. This burns meaningful calories without any recovery cost. Walk after meals, take calls walking, park far away. This alone can be the difference between losing and stalling.

2. **LISS (Low-Intensity Steady State)** — incline treadmill walking, cycling, etc., at a pace where you can still hold a conversation. **2–4 sessions/week of 25–40 min.** Easy on recovery, sustainable, great for an advanced lifter on a cut.

3. **HIIT (High-Intensity Intervals)** — short, hard intervals (e.g., 30 s hard / 90 s easy × 6–8). Time-efficient, but **costly to recovery** when you're already lifting hard and under-fueled. Cap at **1–2 short sessions/week**, and never the day before legs.

### Practical prescription for your cut

```
Start with:  10,000 steps/day  +  2× LISS (30 min)  per week
If you stall: add a 3rd LISS session OR bump steps to 12,000
Avoid:        replacing food cuts with endless cardio — keep some
              "cardio room" to use later when fat loss slows
```

> **Coaching insight:** Don't max out cardio on day one. If you're already doing 60 min/day in week 1, you have nowhere to go when progress stalls in week 8. Keep cardio as a lever you can pull *later*. Prioritize diet cuts and steps first.

### Cardio timing
- **Best:** separate from lifting (different time of day), or **after** lifting — never a hard cardio session *before* you lift heavy.
- **Fasted cardio** offers no meaningful fat-loss advantage over fed cardio. Do it fasted only if you personally prefer it. Total daily calories are what matter.

## Recovery: where the results are actually made

You don't grow in the gym — you grow recovering *from* the gym. On a deficit, recovery is **harder and more important**. This is the lever most people ignore, and it's why they fail.

### 1. Sleep — the king of recovery
- **7–9 hours per night.** Non-negotiable. This is more anabolic than any supplement on earth.
- Poor sleep → higher cortisol, worse insulin sensitivity, more muscle loss, more hunger, worse lifts. It sabotages **every** other lever.
- **Sleep hygiene:** consistent bed/wake times, dark cool room, no screens 30–60 min before bed, cut caffeine ~8 hrs before sleep, limit alcohol (it wrecks deep sleep).

### 2. Stress management
- Chronic stress keeps **cortisol** elevated, which promotes fat retention (especially abdominal) and muscle breakdown.
- Tools: daily walks, 10 min of breathing/meditation, time outdoors, managing workload. On a cut, dieting itself is a stressor — don't pile unnecessary life stress on top.

### 3. Active recovery & mobility
- On your rest day, **move** — a walk, light mobility, stretching. Full couch-rest is fine occasionally but gentle movement aids recovery.
- **5–10 min of mobility** for hips, shoulders, and t-spine keeps the heavy lifts healthy. Joint health is what lets you train hard for years.

### 4. Listen to recovery biofeedback
Watch for the signs you're under-recovering (covered in [`07-tracking.md`](07-tracking.md)): resting heart rate creeping up, lifts dropping for multiple sessions, persistent soreness, bad mood, poor sleep, getting sick. When these stack up → **deload and/or diet break**.

---

## The recovery non-negotiables, ranked

1. **Sleep 7–9 hrs** — fixes almost everything.
2. **Hit protein** — raw material for repair.
3. **Manage stress** — protects hormones.
4. **Deload on schedule** — sheds accumulated fatigue.

Get these right and you'll keep your muscle and your sanity through the entire cut.
