# 03 — Training: The 6-Day Push/Pull/Legs

This is your muscle-retention engine. While dieting, the #1 job of training is to **signal your body to keep every ounce of muscle** — which means lifting heavy and keeping intensity high, even as fuel drops. We do **not** switch to "high-rep toning" circuits on a cut; that's the fastest way to lose muscle. Heavy weight stays heavy.

---

## The split (PPL × 2)

A classic **Push / Pull / Legs**, run twice over 6 days — ideal for an advanced lifter who can train 5–6 days and recover well. Each muscle is hit **2× per week**, which research consistently shows beats 1× for hypertrophy.

```
Day 1: Push (chest, shoulders, triceps)
Day 2: Pull (back, rear delts, biceps)
Day 3: Legs (quads, hams, glutes, calves)
Day 4: Push
Day 5: Pull
Day 6: Legs
Day 7: Rest
```

**Training 5 days?** Run PPL on a rolling basis (Push/Pull/Legs/Push/Pull → rest → Legs/Push/Pull...) so each session still lands ~every 4–5 days. Or do P/P/L/rest/P/P + L floats. The key is hitting each pattern ~2×/week on average.

---

## How to read the prescription

- **Sets × Reps** — working sets (after warm-ups).
- **RIR** = Reps In Reserve = how many reps you stop short of failure. On a cut, **train hard but leave 1–2 RIR on most sets** to manage fatigue; take isolation work closer to failure (0–1 RIR).
- **Rest** — 2–3 min on compounds, 60–90 s on isolation.
- **Tempo** — control the eccentric (lowering) for ~2 seconds. No bouncing, no ego.

**Warm-up every session:** 5 min light cardio, then 2–3 progressively heavier ramp-up sets on your first compound. Never load a heavy bar cold.

## DAY 1 & 4 — PUSH

| # | Exercise | Sets × Reps | RIR | Notes |
|---|----------|-------------|-----|-------|
| 1 | Barbell or Incline Bench Press | 4 × 5–8 | 1–2 | Your heavy strength anchor. Alternate flat/incline between the two push days. |
| 2 | Seated Dumbbell Shoulder Press | 3 × 8–10 | 1–2 | Primary delt builder. |
| 3 | Incline Dumbbell Press | 3 × 10–12 | 1 | Upper chest focus. |
| 4 | Cable Lateral Raise | 4 × 12–15 | 0–1 | Side delts = the "capped shoulder" look. Constant tension. |
| 5 | Overhead Cable Triceps Extension | 3 × 10–12 | 0–1 | Long head of the triceps. |
| 6 | Triceps Pushdown (rope) | 3 × 12–15 | 0–1 | Finisher, squeeze hard. |

---

## DAY 2 & 5 — PULL

| # | Exercise | Sets × Reps | RIR | Notes |
|---|----------|-------------|-----|-------|
| 1 | Weighted Pull-up or Lat Pulldown | 4 × 6–10 | 1–2 | Build the V-taper. Full stretch at the top. |
| 2 | Barbell or Pendlay Row | 4 × 6–8 | 1–2 | Back thickness. Keep a flat spine. |
| 3 | Chest-Supported Row (machine/DB) | 3 × 10–12 | 1 | Mid-back, no lower-back fatigue. |
| 4 | Face Pull | 3 × 15–20 | 0–1 | Rear delts + shoulder health. Do these religiously. |
| 5 | Incline Dumbbell Curl | 3 × 10–12 | 0–1 | Biceps on stretch. |
| 6 | Hammer Curl | 3 × 12–15 | 0–1 | Brachialis + forearm thickness. |

## DAY 3 & 6 — LEGS

| # | Exercise | Sets × Reps | RIR | Notes |
|---|----------|-------------|-----|-------|
| 1 | Back Squat (Day 3) / Romanian Deadlift (Day 6) | 4 × 5–8 | 1–2 | Squat-led one day, hinge-led the other for balanced quad/ham development. |
| 2 | Leg Press | 3 × 10–12 | 1 | Quad volume with less spinal load. |
| 3 | Lying or Seated Leg Curl | 4 × 10–12 | 0–1 | Hamstrings — most people under-train these. |
| 4 | Walking Lunge or Bulgarian Split Squat | 3 × 10–12/leg | 1 | Unilateral strength + glutes. |
| 5 | Leg Extension | 3 × 12–15 | 0–1 | Quad isolation, control the negative. |
| 6 | Standing Calf Raise | 4 × 12–15 | 0–1 | Full stretch + pause at the bottom. |
| 7 | Seated Calf Raise | 3 × 15–20 | 0–1 | Hits the soleus (different from standing). |

## Weekly volume check

This program delivers roughly **14–18 hard sets per muscle group per week** — squarely in the evidence-backed range for hypertrophy in trained lifters. On a deep cut, if recovery suffers, it's fine to trim the *last* isolation set from a couple of exercises. **Cut volume before you cut intensity.**

---

## Form & longevity non-negotiables
- **Full range of motion** beats heavier partials for growth — especially the stretched position.
- **Control the eccentric.** Most growth stimulus and most joint safety live here.
- **Never train through sharp joint pain.** Swap the exercise (e.g., barbell → dumbbell, flat → incline) — there's always an alternative.
- **Warm up the working joints**, not just the muscle.
