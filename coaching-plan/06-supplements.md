# 06 — Supplements: Evidence Over Hype

The supplement industry sells hope. Most products do nothing. A small handful are genuinely backed by strong evidence. Here's the honest tiered breakdown — **nail your diet, training, and sleep first; supplements are the last 5%.**

---

## Tier 1 — Strong evidence, worth your money

| Supplement | Dose | What it actually does |
|------------|------|----------------------|
| **Creatine Monohydrate** | 3–5 g/day, every day | The single most-studied, most-effective legal supplement. Increases strength, power, and muscle. Especially valuable **on a cut** — helps you retain strength and fullness in a deficit. Take it any time; consistency > timing. No need to "load." |
| **Protein powder (whey/casein/plant)** | As needed to hit targets | Not magic — just convenient, cheap protein. Whey post-workout, casein before bed. Use it to fill the gap between food protein and your daily target. |
| **Caffeine** | 100–300 mg pre-workout | Proven performance and focus boost; blunts appetite slightly. Don't take within ~8 hrs of bed. Cycle off occasionally to keep it effective. |
| **Vitamin D3** | 1,000–2,000 IU/day (if deficient) | Most people are low, especially indoors. Supports hormones, bone, immune function. Get bloodwork if unsure. |

---

## Tier 2 — Decent evidence, situational

| Supplement | Dose | When it's worth it |
|------------|------|-------------------|
| **Citrulline Malate** | 6–8 g pre-workout | Modest pump and endurance benefit. Nice-to-have, not essential. |
| **Omega-3 (fish oil)** | 1–3 g EPA+DHA/day | Joint health, recovery, general health — useful if you don't eat fatty fish. |
| **Beta-Alanine** | 3–5 g/day | Helps in the 8–15 rep range (buffers fatigue). Harmless tingling is normal. |
| **Electrolytes (Na/K/Mg)** | As needed | Useful on a cut and with high cardio/sweat — prevents cramps, flat workouts, and headaches from low sodium. |
| **Multivitamin** | 1/day | Cheap insurance against micronutrient gaps while dieting. Not a substitute for vegetables. |

---

## Tier 3 — Skip these (waste of money)

- **BCAAs** — pointless if you already eat enough protein (you do). Whole protein is superior.
- **Fat burners / "thermogenics"** — mostly caffeine + marketing. The fat loss is the caffeine; buy caffeine.
- **Testosterone boosters** (Tribulus, etc.) — don't meaningfully raise testosterone in healthy men.
- **Mass gainers** — just sugar and cheap protein. Irrelevant on a cut anyway.
- **Glutamine, CLA, raspberry ketones, "detox" products** — no meaningful effect for a healthy lifter.

---

## A sensible stack for your cut

```
Daily:
  • Creatine        5 g
  • Vitamin D3      1,000–2,000 IU
  • Whey/casein     to hit protein target
  • Omega-3         1–2 g (optional, if low fish intake)
  • Electrolytes    as needed (esp. with cardio)

Pre-workout:
  • Caffeine        150–250 mg
  • Citrulline      6–8 g (optional)
```

That's it. Everything else is noise. **No supplement will out-perform consistent diet, hard training, and good sleep** — those are the real "performance enhancers."
