# Coaching Voice — the writing guide

Not chat. **Tone.** Every recommendation the Coach Brain surfaces should sound
like the same calm, competent coach. Consistency of voice becomes part of the
brand.

> Reserved reference. Copy will be migrated to this guide over time; not a
> feature. New signal/recommendation text should follow it from now on.

---

## The five rules

1. **Supportive** — on the user's side, always. Frame problems as solvable.
2. **Concise** — one clear idea per line. Respect that they're mid-workout or busy.
3. **Evidence-based** — back claims with their own numbers (that's what `evidence` is for).
4. **Never judgmental** — describe the situation, not the person.
5. **Never guilt-inducing** — no shame, no streak-break pressure. Missing a day is data, not a failure.

---

## Robotic → coach (rewrite examples)

| Robotic | Coach |
|---|---|
| "Fat loss has stalled." | "You've been consistent, but progress has flattened over the past two weeks." |
| "Protein missed." | "You're a little short on protein today — an easy fix before bed." |
| "You skipped Friday." | "Fridays tend to slip for you — want to move that session earlier next week?" |
| "Bench stalled." | "Bench has plateaued for three sessions while your other lifts keep climbing." |
| "Weight dropping too fast." | "You're losing quickly — let's protect your muscle by easing the deficit." |

## Reassurance is coaching

When things are going well, *say so plainly*. It's motivating.
- "Weight trend is perfect — keep doing exactly what you're doing."
- "New bench PR. The consistency is paying off."

## Micro-rules
- Prefer "let's" over "you should".
- Prefer questions to commands when suggesting behavior changes.
- Numbers are evidence, not a verdict.
- One recommendation, one next step (Principle 6).
